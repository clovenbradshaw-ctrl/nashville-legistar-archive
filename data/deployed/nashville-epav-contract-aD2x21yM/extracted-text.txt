MISSION AND PROGRAM STATEMENT 
 
 
MISSION 
Our mission is to train and support Court Appointed Volunteers to advocate for abused and neglected 
children that have come to the attention of Davidson County Juvenile Court so that each child can be safe, 
have a permanent home and the opportunity to thrive. 
 
 
ADVOCACY PROGRAM 
 
Advocacy program volunteers receive 36 hours of training to include instruction on the juvenile 
justice system, state and federal laws, abuse and neglect, substance abuse, resources in the 
community and methods to effectively advocate for a child.  Advocates are appointed by the 
juvenile judge, magistrates, or referees to investigate a case.  Through written reports and 
testimony, volunteers give recommendations as to what they believe is the child’s best interest.

FY26 Metro Nashville Budget Proposal – Funding Request for CASA Nashville 
 
Submitted to: Metro Nashville Women's Caucus 
Primary Contact: Julieanna Huddle, Executive Director 
Organization: CASA Nashville 
Date: 4/28/25 
1. Purpose and Need 
CASA Nashville respectfully requests an investment of $75,000 through the FY26 Metro Nashville Operating 
Budget to expand our critical advocacy services for children and youth in foster care across Davidson County. This 
investment will increase CASA’s capacity by 11%, allowing us to serve up to 400 children annually, which 
represents 56% of the estimated 715 children in foster care on any given day in Davidson County.  
 
CASA Nashville is the only organization in Davidson County providing individualized, court-appointed advocacy to 
children navigating the foster care and court systems. Our specially trained volunteer advocates help ensure 
children receive the educational, medical, and emotional support they need to thrive. 
 
This funding is urgently needed to meet rising demand and address the overrepresentation of Black and Hispanic 
children in foster care—who comprise 75% of CASA’s caseload, despite representing only 34% of the general 
population. Our trauma-informed advocacy is designed to reduce disparities and create long-term stability for our 
community’s most vulnerable youth, encouraging connection and belonging for every child and family served by 
CASA Nashville.

PURPOSE 
 
CASA Nashville recruits and trains Volunteer Advocates who are appointed by the Juvenile Court to represent children in cases that 
have come to the Court’s attention due to child abuse and neglect. Our staff Team Leaders coach and empower the Volunteer 
Advocates in their work with children and families. CASA Volunteer Advocates are child-centered and family-focused and often 
remain the one consistent for children in foster care. CASA Nashville employs staff Team Leaders in Specialist roles to support the 
work of Volunteer Advocates in order to further specialize in each child’s specific needs. The current specializations include: Safe 
Babies Court; Immigrant and Hispanic Families; and Adolescent Services. Further specialisms are being developed and may include 
addressing educational gaps for foster children, assisting older youth who are likely to age out of the system, achieving timely 
permanency, and addressing complex, serious childhood trauma.  
 
Where possible, the initial goal of each CASA Volunteer Advocate is to ascertain biological family strengths and needs, and to 
recommend to the Court plans and services by which a child may be safely and successfully reunited with their family of origin. In 
situations where reunification is not possible, a CASA Volunteer Advocate represents the best interests of the child in securing home 
placement with an appropriate extended family member or a suitable permanent guardianship. If there is no safe and loving 
permanent home available with a relative or guardian, the CASA Volunteer Advocate will assist in advocating for the child to be 
made eligible for adoption so they can find their forever home as quickly as possible. 
 
Goal 1: CASA will assess the needs of child victims of abuse and neglect and advocate on their behalf throughout the permanency 
placement process in Juvenile Court. 
 
 
Objective 1: Assess the impact CASA can make for every case referred by the Davidson County Juvenile Court and serve as many 
children victims of abuse and neglect as capacity allows. 
 
Objective 2: CASA Volunteer Advocates will thoroughly assess each assigned child’s and family’s specific needs so that appropriate 
social service resources can be recommended and implemented in a timely manner by DCS. 
 
Objective 3: CASA Volunteer Advocates will carefully and thoroughly evaluate each assigned case and report recommendations to 
the court so that the best interests of the child can be determined by the judge.  
 
Goal 2:  CASA will expand its pool of highly-trained Volunteer Advocates to provide CASA advocacy services. 
 
Objective 1: CASA will recruit, interview and background check highly dedicated volunteers who will receive extensive 36-hours of 
training/on-boarding to provide best interest advocacy for children and youth placed in the foster care system through no fault of 
their own.  
 
Objective 2:  CASA Team Leaders and program staff will provide continued education to further development of current Volunteer 
Advocates with skills in Educational Advocacy, Advocating for Older Youth, Dealing with Complex, Serious Trauma, and Achieving 
Permanency/Wrap Around Support for Families. 
 
Objective 3: CASA will recruit and train the maximum number of Volunteer Advocates allowed by National CASA Standards (i.e., 
maximum of 30 Volunteer Advocates per each full-time staff member, and 10 Volunteer Advocates per each Peer Coordinator)

Activity 1.1a: CASA Nashville staff will review all referrals from Juvenile Court and assess them against an Impact of

Activity 1.1b: CASA will assign a volunteer or staff person to all cases in which CASA deems it can make the greatest impact

Objective 2: CASA Volunteer Advocates will assure that each assigned child gets the social service resources they need. 
Activity 1.2a: CASA Volunteer Advocates will review each assigned case and participate in the creation of a permanency 
plan to make recommendations for services.  
Activity 1.2b: CASA advocates will meet with DCS case workers, school officers, and others to ensure that the social services 
needed are engaged by the appropriate agency or service provider.  
 
Objective 3: CASA Volunteer Advocates will carefully and thoroughly evaluate each assigned case and report their 
recommendations on the best interests of the child to the court to inform the judge/magistrate’s decision making.  
 
Activity 1.3a: The CASA Volunteer Advocate summarizes the findings of their case work into the impacts of the child’s 
trauma (physical, mental, verbal, or sexual abuse; neglect; witness to abuse, exposure to drug use; etc.); social services and mental 
health needs; and permanency recommendations; and organizes them into a comprehensive report submitted through verbal 
statements and/or written reports to the court. Included in these reports, CASA Volunteer Advocates also present the child's wishes 
to the court, even if the Volunteer Advocate doesn't agree with them, as the role of CASA is to be the child's voice in the court 
proceedings. 
 
 
Activity 1.3b: The CASA Volunteer Advocate presents recommendations (via verbal statements and/or written reports) to 
the Juvenile Court Judge and/or Magistrate presiding over the case so that the judge has the most accurate picture of the child’s 
circumstances and can make a decision in the child’s best interest. 
 
Goal 2:  CASA will recruit and maintain highly-trained Volunteer Advocates to provide CASA advocacy services. 
 
Objective 1: Highly experienced and seasoned CASA volunteers will receive additional training to meet continued education 
requirements by National CASA standards.   
Activity 2.1a: CASA Volunteers will complete 12 hours of additional training on policies and procedures, leadership, 
coaching, and working with specific clientele. 
 
Activity 2.1b: Peer Coordinators (seasoned CASA Volunteers who provide supervision to new volunteers) will be required to 
complete 12 additional training hours annually, and specialized training for their leadership role will be offered. They will also attend 
regular, frequent staff meetings, events, and training courses.  
 
 
Objective 2: CASA staff Team Leaders will be trained in Specialist roles to support the work of Volunteer Advocates. 
Activity 2.2a: One CASA staff member per special skill trained in order to cascade advocacy skills to Volunteer Advocates so 
that they can meet the unique needs of children in the Juvenile Court system. 
 
Activity 2.2b: CASA Team Leaders will host specialized training for other staff and volunteers in their area of specialized 
focus, which will help them advocate more effectively for the needs of children in the Juvenile Court system. These sessions may be 
delivered through Volunteer Advocate team meetings, lunch and learns, and quarterly mini summits, which will consist of half-day 
training sessions, and an annual all-day Volunteer Summit, using an internally developed curriculum. 
 
Objective 3: Recruit and support a diverse, well trained, and empowered group of effective child advocates to represent the 
culturally and racially diverse community we serve. 
Activity 2.3a: CASA revises recruitment materials and processes to appeal to a more diverse population, including updating 
CASA’s website to be more mobile user-friendly, allowing for foreign language interface, and support on-going training needs for our 
diverse volunteer pool.  
Activity 2.3b: CASA continues to implement a Volunteer Team model to empower Volunteer Advocates and provide peer 
support, peer relationship opportunities, training, coaching, and mentoring across the agency.

Primary Inputs: CASA Nashville has one focus—Volunteer Advocacy for child victims of abuse and neglect as detailed in this

Volunteer Advocate hours annually, and CASA staff time not funded by this grant. 
 
Inputs also include relationships with our collaborative partners including Juvenile Court, DCS, and multiple social service and 
government agencies. Juvenile Court refers all children in DCS custody to CASA at the beginning of each child’s case so that CASA 
may determine if the case is appropriate to staff with a CASA volunteer. (According to Nashville CASA policies and current practices, 
a child must have been removed from a home by DCS due to child victimization and placed in state custody.) CASA staff and 
volunteers then work closely with Juvenile Court staff, court-appointed attorneys, Foster Care Review Boards, and the Department 
of Children's Services to ensure the safe and expedited permanent placement of the abused and neglected children we serve. CASA 
also works with community agencies such as health care providers, schools, and partner nonprofits and agencies that provide 
services to the children they serve. 
 
 
DATA COLLECTION PROCEDURE 
 
CASA Nashville has been tracking and reporting client and program data since it was founded more than 40 years ago and has 
multiple funders and agencies to which it is accountable, including state and National CASA, United Way, and private foundations. 
These include tracking what percentage of our advocate recommendations are accepted by the court, and what percentage of 
clients served exit foster care in accordance with CASA recommendations, and what percentage of clients served remain in stable 
placements for at least six months (six months is the critical window for permanency success, as determined by National CASA 
Association research).  
 
CASA uses Optima, a CASA-specific data tracking program, to track individual cases and case notes, numbers of children served, case 
outcomes, and various other data. We also use Optima to track and monitor trends across our client population and identify new 
areas for service and advocacy. Data from Optima is used in a PQI process designed to identify trends, measure progress toward 
goals and objectives, and identify and ameliorate problems.  
 
How CASA works within the Juvenile Court System: Typically, a child’s case is referred to CASA by the magistrate at the Juvenile 
Court. Prior to CASA’s involvement, the child has been placed in state custody through involvement of the Tennessee Department of 
Children’s Services (DCS), usually due to allegations of abuse or neglect. Once CASA receives the referral, CASA staff review the case 
to determine how to best serve the child and assessing the impact our advocacy might have on achieving permanency for the child. 
Depending on capacity and the severity of other cases referred, CASA either assigns a CASA Volunteer Advocate, flags the case for a 
brief period of staff monitoring, or determines the case will not be served by CASA at that time. Cases are assigned during bi-weekly 
Program Team meetings and decisions are informed by initial investigations. Once a case has been assigned to a CASA volunteer, 
that volunteer meets with the child, the family, DCS, medical providers, school personnel and any other relevant parties to gain an 
in-depth understanding of that child’s needs and helps develop recommendations for the court and the Child and Family Team that 
reflect the best interests of that child or children. The advocate serves as a coordinating presence between all the different agencies 
involved in that child’s plan to give a voice to that child’s needs and make sure all issues are addressed. Throughout this process, all 
relevant data is recorded into the client case file in Optima, including all interactions the Volunteer Advocate has with the child to 
build a relationship and gain an understanding of that child's needs and wishes. 
 
Once a plan has been created for a child with DCS and all parties in the case, there are typically several goals the child, the family, 
and each agency need to meet before the child can be returned to the home, placed with a relative, placed with a permanent 
guardian, or be adopted. These goals may include putting services in place, addressing issues in the home, starting therapy or 
parenting classes – anything that may be necessary for the child to be successful in the care of his or her biological family. This plan 
is then approved by the court, with recommendations from the CASA volunteer. The Volunteer Advocate stays with the child 
throughout this process, ensuring that barriers are addressed, and goals are met. Once the goals have been met, the child can exit 
state custody and be placed permanently in a safe, loving home. Again, all activities and progress toward goals are recorded in the 
client case file in Optima.  CASA tracks all incoming referrals to ensure that children are not re-entering care during the 6 months 
after case closure and permanency, which is deemed the critical time to establish permanency by National CASA.
