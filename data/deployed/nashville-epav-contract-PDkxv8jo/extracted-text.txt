The parties hereby agree to the following terms and conditions: 
 
I. 
Duties and Responsibilities of ARTIST.    
Artist agrees to provide and METRO agrees to purchase the following services:  
The Artist will collaborate with the Metro Park’s design team on the design of 
Southeast Park (hereafter the “Park”) as the whole project have been described in 
the Call to Artist and in the Artist’s answer and proposal (hereafter the “Project”).  
 
During the Project, Artist will also serve a mentorship role to a local artist interested 
in expanding its knowledge and experience in public art. This will require 
developing a scope for mentee artist and participating in a mentee selection 
process guided by Metro Arts, and will require at least monthly remote or in-person 
check-ins with mentee during duration of the project, once mentee is selected.  
 
The Project consists of three (3) phases: 
● 
Phase I includes design team and community engagement and artwork 
concept development/schematic design, mentee scoping and selection 
process: 
A/ 
Upon contract execution, Artist will prepare and submit a Community 
Engagement Work Plan. Metro Arts will review take up to three (3) weeks to 
approve and if approved, ARTIST will conduct the community engagement work 
for the Project.  
B/ 
Upon completing the Community Engagement Work Plan, the Artist will be 
expected to design, fabricate and install at least one permanent public artwork, 
either free-standing or integrated into the infrastructure of the Park (hereafter the 
“Artwork”). For this purpose, Artist will develop a design concept  for the Artwork 
(also called schematic design) (hereafter the “Design Concept” or the Schematic 
Design”). The design development process will include site visits and/or meeting 
and coordination with Metro Arts staff, local citizens and design team.

EXHIBIT E 
Artist Qualifications Proposal  
 
More together than alone 
 
  
Daily tous les jours leads multidisciplinary projects at the intersection of interactive art 
and narrative environments. 
Our projects enchant everyday life, igniting in people a sense of what is possible. We 
explore the future of 
cities, new ways to tell stories, collaborate, and harness the power of collectivity. These 
interventions are opportunities to enable change, stimulate engagement that sparks 
conversation amongst strangers, and create strong bonds between citizens and their 
environment. 
 
Our projects aim to empower people in the stories that are told around them. 
For example: A building hosts a radio show, telling tales generated by its inhabitants 
(Amateur Intelligence Radio, Saint Paul); Passers-by move like stars and planets 
to animate a planetarium’s facade (Choreographies For Humans & Stars, Montréal); 
Musical traffic lights for pedestrians bridge two sides of a busy street (Knock Stop Music, 
San Francisco). 
 
Placemaking 
 
We first discovered the power of turning spaces into places with our 
21 musical swings project (21 Balançoires). The giant human-powered musical 
instrument has become a symbol of Montréal’s entertainment district. Beyond its strong 
visual presence, both day and night, it brings a new set of rituals to the

Narrative Environments 
 
Every space tells a story. Our projects begin with an in-depth understanding of the 
existing or desired identity of a location and its people. We learn about the people in 
these public spaces—where they come from, what they aspire to, how they like to spend 
their days. A project’s narrative takes shape through interaction and evolves as people 
experience it—this a common thread in our work. 
 
Technology 
 
We are fascinated by technology’s capacity to engage people, strengthen a sense of 
collectivity, and improve how we relate to public environments. Technology can help 
create magic. It can also be a great distraction: A project’s context dictates if we choose 
to use technology, and if so, how it is integrated to the environment. We 
also work with local team to ensure that sufficient knowledge exists on the ground to 
bring a project to life, and sustain it over time. 
 
Integration 
 
We have created numerous public artworks involving large multi-disciplinary teams. We 
work closely with architects, structural and mechanical engineers, contractors and city 
representatives to make a vision happen on the ground.

Metro Arts Credit Policy 
 
Credit & Logo Usage Guidelines  
Metro Arts’ funding programs distribute public funds that have been approved by citizen 
panelists, Metro Nashville Arts Commission, and Metro Council to support arts and culture 
programming in Metro Nashville-Davidson County. It is imperative that all Davidson County 
residents, businesses, and elected officials are kept informed of how these funds are being used, 
and that they understand the significant impact these funds have on creating a vibrant arts 
community. To that end, all recipients of Metro Arts funding must take an active role in publicly 
providing credit or acknowledgment for the source of that funding.  
 
In all publicity, promotions, online engagement, and/or printed materials related to the funded 
project or programs, recipients are required to credit Metro Arts including detailed recognition 
and specific logo placement as appropriate. This public acknowledgement must include 
prominent display of the Metro Arts logo and/or approved text that explicitly states that Metro Arts 
supports the organization and/or specific project. If using the text option, acceptable titles are 
Metro Arts or Metro Arts / Nashville Office of Art + Culture. This acknowledgement must 
be included in, but is not limited to, news releases, marketing materials, advertisements, 
publications and other materials offered to the public, signs or notices on or around a 
business, studio, or organization’s base of operations, web sites, online communication, 
speeches, and public appearances. Metro Arts Logo Usage and Style Guide and logo files can 
be found on our website on the Arts Resources and Toolkits page. 
 
Project Support Recipients (Learning Lab, Public Art, and Project Grants) 
This policy applies to all publicity, promotions, online engagement, and/or printed materials 
related to the funded project only. In press communications, please use the following approved 
language: This project is funded in part by Metro Arts. Please credit Metro Arts through logo

Exhibit H 
 
Definitions 
 
Design Team: The design team includes individuals that are directly involved with the Southeast 
Davidson Regional Park project from Metro Parks, Metro Arts, Collier Engineering, Hodgson-
Douglas Landscape Architects, Tara Armistead Landscape Architects, and Nelson, Byrd, Woltz 
Landscape Architects and Daily tous les jours. Metro Arts will make every effort to inform Artist of 
any changes to the design team. 
Community Engagement Work Plan: The Community Engagement Plan should include a 
written document of how the artist(s) will collect feedback and information from the community. It 
should also outline a plan for public meetings, events or presentations that the artist will conduct. 
Metro Arts will assist with meeting location identification and coordination as well as promotion of 
engagement events. 
Community Engagement Phase: the community engagement phase of the project is the phase 
in which the artist(s) will explore the community and its citizens to collect information and 
feedback that may inform the design of the public artwork. 
Schematic Design: The schematic design is a rough draft of drawings that clearly illustrate the 
concept of the artwork. The schematic design drawings are not as detailed as construction 
drawings and will not be used during the fabrication and installation of the artwork, but will be 
used to show scale and spatial relationships.  
Final Design Proposal: The Final Design Proposal is the final approved artwork design that has 
been structurally engineered and for which stamped/engineered structural drawings have been 
submitted.
