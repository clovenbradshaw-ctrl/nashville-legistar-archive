Nashville Farmers’ Market  
Market House Tenant Rules and Policies Handbook 
 
Updated February 2024 
 
Farmers, makers, and small businesses are the cornerstone of the Nashville Farmers' Market (referred to as the 
market in the remainder of this document). Since its inception in the 1800s, the market has offered these small 
businesses the opportunity to vend more than 350 days a year.  
 
Nashville Farmers' Market tenant benefits: 
 
● 
A welcoming, diverse community of tenants and supporters 
● 
More than 1,500 surface parking spaces conveniently located within half a mile 
● 
Opportunity to be featured in seasonal events, workshops, advertising, etc. 
● 
Support from market staff to help promote business efforts. 
 
About the Nashville Farmers' Market: The market operates daily as a retail hub and small business incubation 
center for farmers, artisans, and small businesses that desire to serve the region. The market is located on a 12-
acre property in the urban core of Nashville, within walking distance of many local neighborhoods, including 
Germantown, Salemtown, the Gulch and the downtown district. The public facility includes two outdoor sheds, a 
garden center, and a food hall with roughly 25 diverse dining options and retail stores. 
 
Mission statement: Curating an inclusive destination marketplace that fosters a connection between our 
community and the farmers, foods, and artisans who contribute to our food system.

Application and rates 
 
How to apply  
 
1. Read this handbook  in full before applying. Submitting your application means you agree to comply with 
the rules and policies set forth in this handbook, in addition to all applicable rules. You may review the 
Nashville Farmers’ Market rules by clicking here.  
2. Apply by clicking here. 
3. Upload the required documents for your vendor type as listed in the application. 
4. Answer all questions for approval. 
5. Review your application (incomplete applications will not be considered). 
 
Market management may request additional information, a meeting, and/or product samples if considering your 
application for approval. After an internal review of applications, market staff will select a finalist to make a 
presentation to the Nashville Farmers’ Market board during a public meeting. 
 
Please note: the application process at the Nashville Farmers’ Market is competitive. Acceptance of tenants 
will be at the discretion of market management and the board. Applications will be approved as needed by the 
market’s management team based on criteria including, but not limited to: 
 
● 
Product offerings should diversify current offerings 
● 
Product assortment should represent the city and culture of Nashville and / or the TN region  
● 
Restaurant menus should reflect locally and regionally sourced food; healthy options preferred 
● 
Tenants must source and utilize green / sustainable materials and support ongoing recycling  
 efforts of the market 
● 
Locally-owned businesses are preferred 
● 
Market-readiness including marketing, branding and merchandising 
● 
A track record of a strong business plan and financials, marketing experience, positive conduct,  
 adherence to policies, attendance records, if applicable, etc. 
 
Market management reserves the right to deny the application of any tenant for any reason other than 
discrimination, as outlined in the Metropolitan Government’s non-discrimination policy, set forth on page 13. 
 
Required documents 
 
Insurance: All tenants must carry general liability insurance, including product liability insurance, and property 
insurance at a minimum of $1 million. Tenants must carry liability insurance for vehicles owned, non-owned, and 
hired with a minimum of $1 million and include coverage for loading and unloading hazards. Tenants shall 
maintain workers’ compensation insurance with statutory limits required by the State of Tennessee or other 
applicable laws and Employer’s Liability Insurance with limits of no less than one hundred thousand dollars 
($100,000) as required by the laws of Tennessee, except such insurance is not required for companies with fewer 
than five employees.  Liquor Liability Insurance shall be in full force and effect IF Lessee will serve alcoholic 
beverages. Tenant’s insurance shall name Metropolitan Government of Nashville and Davidson County and the 
State of Tennessee as additional insured.  All tenants shall maintain all other coverage as required by law. Copies 
of current certificates of insurance must be provided with the application. If insurance expires during the season,

Staff and tenant services 
 
Market management: The market’s management team is responsible for administering and enforcing all market 
rules, policies and procedures, as well as ensuring public safety, hosting market kitchen demonstrations and 
managing market events and festivals. Market management is instructed to handle and resolve any issues that 
may arise during a market day, and all market staff members have authority to resolve issues in a civil and 
efficient manner.  The current management team and staff are: 
 
Darrell Lane, Executive Director: darrell.lane@nashville.gov

Marketing and advertising: The market uses its marketing, advertising and social media tools to support the 
events and operations of the market as a whole. All tenants grant the market an unrestricted right and license to 
use their image, likeness, actions, and statements in any photograph, broadcast, telecast, social media post, or 
other transmission or reproduction of market premises without further authorization or compensation. 
 
As independent businesses, tenants are expected to execute their own marketing and social media plans. Market 
management uses marketing funds and assets, ads, newsletters, blogs and social media at its discretion. 
However, tagging market social media accounts and informing the market's marketing manager about special 
deals, events, and products that tenants may have can increase inclusion in market-related media. For more 
information about marketing, please reach out to Courtney Cotton, Marketing Manager, at 
courtney.cotton@nashville.gov.  
 
Newsletters: Market management uses emailed newsletters to communicate with market house tenants as well 
as the community at large. These regularly emailed newsletters include information about upcoming events, 
promotions and more. Merchants are responsible for reviewing these newsletters to stay informed regarding 
market happenings. 
 
Slack: The market utilizes Slack to communicate quick, day-to-day information across the market, including 
weather and facility information, team collaboration and more. Slack is a free messaging app available for Android 
and Apple users. All tenants are invited and encouraged to join by emailing Courtney Cotton, Marketing Manager, 
at courtney.cotton@nashville.gov.

member of the security team immediately by calling or texting (615) 710-3578.

Payment 
 
Advance payment: All tenants are expected to make payment by the 5th of each calendar month using a check, 
money order, or other means approved by the market’s finance office. Checks can be mailed to: 
Nashville Farmers’ Market 
Attn: Finance Office 
900 Rosa L. Parks Blvd. 
Nashville, TN 37208 
 
No cash payments will be accepted. Checks and money orders can be mailed or dropped off in person at the 
market’s management office between 9 a.m. and 3 p.m. on Tuesday – Friday. After hours check payments can be 
made utilizing the safe drop box outside the office door. Checks are only acceptable from tenant accounts in good 
standing. Returned / bounced checks are automatically sent to Metropolitan Government of Nashville and 
Davidson County's collections department. 
 
It is the tenant’s responsibility to remain in good standing. If a payment is made after the 5th of each month a $50 
late charge will be applied to your account. Past due balances that roll into the following month are subject to a 
$250 late charge, as and for liquidated damages. 
 
All other charges, including any late charges, penalties for violations, or payment for property damage shall also 
be due by the 5th of the month following notice of such charges.  
 
If you are having issues paying your rent, please reach out to the finance office prior to missing a payment. 
Please contact David Griffin, Finance Manager, at david.griffin@nashville.gov for questions or payment 
information.

Market house vending requirements 
 
Market house days and hours of operation 
 
● 
Building hours: 8 a.m. - 8 p.m.  
● 
Tenant required operating hours: 11 a.m. - 4 p.m., seven days per week, excluding the market’s observed 
holidays (New Year’s Eve, New Year’s Day, Thanksgiving Day, and Christmas Day) 
● 
Market house closures: The market may host events in the common areas of the market. The market may 
close at 3 p.m., up to eight days each year, for the purpose of hosting event rentals. The market may also 
schedule additional event rentals to begin at 5 p.m. on other days throughout the year.  Setup for event 
rentals may occur during regular business operating hours.  The market shall provide the tenant with 14 
days’ notice of any event rental.  Whether the tenant must close operations or remain open for business 
during event rentals shall be at the market’s sole determination. 
 
All tenants are required to operate their businesses on all days the market is open to the public unless otherwise 
communicated to and approved by market management. Tenants who close their business during required 
operating hours without prior permission from market management are subject to a $50 fine per day of non-
approved business closure. Approved closures include maintenance or equipment failures, facility issues and 
market-wide closures. 
 
Tenant premises and use 
 
 
● 
Tenant operations must stay within boundaries of their rented, assigned space(s). The placement of 
tenant merchandise or other items in the aisles or anywhere outside the rented space(s) is prohibited, 
unless approved by market management.  
● 
Blocking a roadway with merchandise, equipment or for loading/unloading purposes is strictly prohibited.  
● 
In-house walkways must remain clear at all times for customers during operating hours.  
 
Vendor kiosk carts: Rolling vendor kiosk carts are available for rent inside the market house. A cart membership 
rental requires a three-month commitment, with required operating days and hours of Friday, Saturday, and 
Sunday from 10 a.m. - 4 p.m., excluding market-approved holidays. Kiosk carts are assigned designated spaces 
in the market house. Market management considers a variety of factors in determining cart placements, including 
visibility for your business, as well as consideration of what may disadvantage or block other businesses. 
Relocation of carts is not permitted.  
 
Kiosk cart renters are permitted to add a magnet to the kiosk for branding and are welcome to remove any 
shelves or fixtures that are not of use. Please secure storage of any of these fixtures inside of the cart. Kiosk cart 
renters are responsible for loading, storing, and securing products for sale on carts. Renters will also be provided 
with a cart cover, but each renter will need to supply their own lock. Each kiosk cart comes with a remote control 
that operates cart lighting. Market management provides AAA batteries for these remotes. Kiosk cart renters are 
permitted the use of one bar stool for sitting. No merchandise is permitted on the floor area around the kiosk cart. 
If you need assistance with kiosk cart tires, or have any other kiosk cart questions, please contact Charles Kizer, 
Facilities Manager, at charles.kizer@nashville.gov.

Inclement weather policy: The market is open rain or shine.  Market management will make decisions to alter 
market operations and hours based on weather reports and on-site market conditions. It is within the authority of 
market staff to close the market early, open the market late, or cancel a market day due to severe or inclement 
weather. During inclement weather events, it is up to the individual tenants to weigh factors and make a decision 
about whether to attend the market. Market closures will not count against market attendance records. Rent 
refunds and credits will not be provided for inclement weather closure except with written approval from 
management. 
 
Sales reporting: Monthly net sales are required to be submitted to the market's finance office by the 15th day of 
each calendar month for the prior month. Repeated withholding of net sales information constitutes a policy 
violation. The market reserves the right to conduct audits on sales reporting without notice. 
 
Food containers: Tenants shall only use market-approved food containers while in operation. 
 
Alcoholic beverages and other controlled substances: At all times and in all instances, the sale and 
consumption of alcoholic beverages at the market shall be in accordance with the approval and regulations of all 
state and local government regulatory agencies charged with the oversight thereof. Additionally, no alcoholic 
beverages shall be kept, bought, or sold in any rental space or other market location except pursuant to a current 
lease or as may be approved for an event. Consumption of alcoholic beverages is prohibited by any vendor or 
vendor’s staff during scheduled time of employment. Market approval relating to alcoholic beverages shall not 
indicate or otherwise imply regulatory approval or compliance, which is not within market purview. No illegal 
substances shall be consumed, kept, bought, or sold in any rental space or other market location.

Definitions of harassment 
 
Sexual harassment constitutes discrimination and is illegal under federal, state and local laws. For the purposes 
of this policy, sexual harassment is defined, as in the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Guidelines, as 
unwelcome sexual advances, requests for sexual favors and other verbal or physical conduct of a sexual nature 
when, for example: 
 
● 
Submission to such conduct is made either explicitly or implicitly a term or condition of an  
individual's employment 
● 
Submission to or rejection of such conduct by an individual is used as the basis for employment  
decisions affecting such individuals 
● 
Such conduct has the purpose or effect of unreasonably interfering with an individual's work  
performance or creating an intimidating, hostile or offensive working environment. 
 
Sexual harassment may include a range of subtle and not-so-subtle behaviors and may involve individuals of the 
same or different genders. Depending on the circumstances, these behaviors may include unwanted sexual 
advances or requests for sexual favors; sexual jokes and innuendo; verbal abuse of a sexual nature; commentary 
about an individual's body, sexual prowess or sexual deficiencies; leering, whistling or touching; insulting or 
obscene comments or gestures; display in the workplace of sexually suggestive objects or pictures; and other 
physical, verbal or visual conduct of a sexual nature. 
  
Harassment on the basis of any other protected characteristic is also strictly prohibited. Under this policy, 
harassment is verbal, written or physical conduct that denigrates or shows hostility or aversion toward an 
individual because of their race, color, religion, gender, sexual orientation, national origin, immigration status or 
citizenship, age, disability, marital status, genetic information, veteran status, or any other characteristic protected 
by law or that of their relatives, friends or associates, and that: a) has the purpose or effect of creating an 
intimidating, hostile or offensive work environment; b) has the purpose or effect of unreasonably interfering with 
an individual's work performance; or c) otherwise adversely affects an individual's employment opportunities.  
 
Harassing conduct includes epithets, slurs or negative stereotyping; threatening, intimidating or hostile acts; 
denigrating jokes; and written or graphic material that denigrates or shows hostility or aversion toward an 
individual or group and that is placed on walls or elsewhere on the employer's premises or circulated in the 
workplace, on company time or using company equipment via email, phone (including voice messages), text 
messages, tweets, blogs, social networking sites, or other means.  
 
These policies apply to all tenants, employees and off-site markets where the market operates, whether related to 
conduct engaged in by fellow employees or someone not directly connected to the market (e.g., an outside 
vendor, consultant or customer). Conduct prohibited by these policies is unacceptable in the workplace and in any 
work-related setting outside the workplace, such as during business trips, business meetings and business-
related social events.

Agreement and penalties 
 
As a condition of submitting an application and vending at the market, tenants agree to comply with the rules and 
policy handbook and with all applicable Nashville Farmers’ Market rules, which can be found by clicking here.  
 
Violations and penalties: Violations or failure to comply with any market rules and policies will generally result in 
the following disciplinary action from market management: 
 
● 
First offense: A verbal warning with documentation of the policy offense 
● 
Second offense: A written warning and a $50 policy violation fee per offense 
● 
Third offense: Suspension for a period of time, designated by market management 
● 
Fourth offense: Termination of license / lease agreement, with 30 days’ written notice 
 
Penalties depend on the severity of the offense, and market management may apply such penalties at its 
discretion. All violations of market rules, policies or directives constitute a breach of license to operate at the 
market. Extreme violations can result in the permanent loss of any or all market privileges and contract 
termination, as well as associated charges, fees, and damages. 
 
Amending policies: Market policies may be altered, amended, or replaced by a majority vote of the Nashville 
Farmers’ Market board at any regular or special meeting. Market management may propose changes to the 
market rules and policies and must be filed with the Board chair in writing at least seven days before the meeting 
during which it is to be considered and before the notice of such meeting has been provided. 
 
Indemnity agreement: In consideration of the Nashville Farmers Market granting a license to rent, occupy, and

from and against any and all claims, demands, actions, losses, obligations, costs, charges, expenses, judgments, 
damages, and liabilities whatsoever (including, without limitation, reasonable fees and expenses of attorneys, 
expert witnesses, and other consultants), which the market may sustain, suffer, or incur by reason of, in 
connection with, or arising out of the activities or use of the market facilities by a tenant or its employees, family, 
agents, contractors, or invitees. All tenants agree to hold the market in the event of any damage to or loss of 
personal property brought onto or located on the premises and understand that they bring such property onto the 
premises at their own risk. Tenants are responsible for their own general and product liability insurance.  
 
In the event that a tenant fails to indemnify and hold harmless as herein agreed, the Nashville Farmers’ Market

to recourse against such tenant for all fees, costs, expenses, and payments made or agreed to be paid to 
discharge said claim. In the event of default, tenants agree to pay all reasonable legal fees and expenses
