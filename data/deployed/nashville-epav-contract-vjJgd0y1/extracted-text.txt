GRANT CONTRACT
BETWEEN THE METROPOLITAN GOVERNMENT
OF NASHVILLE AND DAVIDSON COUNTY
AND
Historic Travelers Rest Plantation and Museum

This Grant Contract issued and entered into pursuant to RS2013-881 by and between the Metropolitan

Government of Nashville and Davidson County, a municipal corporation of the State of Tennessee

hereinafter referred to as “Metro”, and Historic Travelers Rest Plantation and Museum, hereinafter

referred to as the “Recipient,” is for the provision of, “providing a heirloom historic garden to reflect the
early 19" century America garden.” as further defined in the "SCOPE OF PROGRAM." The recipient's
audit is incorporated herein by reference.

A. SCOPE OF PROGRAM:

A.1.__ The Recipient shall use the funds to provide services to the citizens of Davidson County.
Incorporated into this contract is the program described in Attachment 1, the application
submitted by the Recipient and any of its amendments, which is attached hereto and made a part
thereof. The Recipient shall work toward the following outcomes:

“To purchase the needed supplies to build and maintain a historic herb garden to fit more
accurately within the time frame of the site and to better share heirloom gardening practices.”

A.2. These funds shall be expended consistent with the proposed budget, included in Attachment 1.
The Recipient shall collect data to evaluate the effectiveness of their services and provide those
results to Metro as requested.

A.3. _ The recipient shall utilize Metro funding only for services the Recipient provides to documented
residents of Davidson County. Documentation of residency shall be established with a recent
utility bill; voter's registration card; driver's license or other government issued-ID; current record
from a school showing address; affidavit by landlord; or affidavit by a nonprofit treatment, shelter,
half-way house, or homeless assistance entity located within Davidson County. Utilization of
Metro funding for services to non-Davidson County residents is unallowable.

A.4. Additionally, the recipient shall collect data on the primary county of residence of the clients it
serves and provide that data to Metro, upon request.

B. GRANT CONTRACT TERM:

B.1. Grant Contract Term. The term of this Grant shall be for a period of ten (10) months,
commencing on July 1, 2013 and ending on April 30, 2014. Metro shall have no obligation for
services rendered by the Recipient which are not performed within this term.

to the Recipient by Metro for all of the Recipient's obligations hereunder. The proposed budget
line items include, but are not limited to, all applicable taxes, fees, overhead, and all other direct
and indirect costs incurred or to be incurred by the Recipient.

Please answer the six questions below. (Form will expand or you may attach additional
sheets.)
1. Provide a brief summary of what you are proposing and include the vision for the
project.
This project will re-interpret our historic herb garden to fit more accurately within the time
frame of the site and to better share heirloom gardening practices, knowledge and lore with
the community. We envision a historic herb garden that will give an accurate representation
of the medicinal, culinary and dye herbs used in early 19" century America. Every effort will
be made to utilize heirloom plants for historical accuracy. In addition, the project will include
research-based interpretation that will share herb history and knowledge through education
programming. The amount requested will cover signage for interpretation and identification
of herbs as well as providing historically accurate tools for garden maintenance. The
signage and tools will be used as part of an education program targeting Middle Tennessee
schools. Travellers Rest will match the grant funds with a free garden event for the
community, staff hours and planning, as well as discounted Title 1 admission to garden-
focused field trips. The Tyne-Meade Garden Club will provide on-going volunteer support
to facilitate planning, research, implementation, maintenance, and will be key to the
success of the project.
2. Provide a garden development and implementation schedule, including the items to
be grown and estimated timeline for harvesting.
With the assistance of the Tyne-Meade Garden Club we hope to have a garden guide
(brochure) and interpretive and identification signage by the end of the year.
Please see the attached garden and maintenance schedule for more detailed
information.

3. Provide a plan for the ongoing maintenance of the garden and its long term stability.
Volunteers from both the Tyne-Meade Garden Club and Hands-On Nashville will be an
important part of the ongoing maintenance and stability of the garden. To date, the Tyne-
Meade Garden Club has donated 80+ hours of on-site work time and 50+ hours in plant
research. In addition, staff and board investment in this project will ensure its long-term
success. Furthermore, as we move the garden to a core position in the interpretation and
history of the site it will be woven into the fabric of our “story”. The staff, board and Colonial
Dames of America currently serve as stewards of the history, collections, historic house and
museum that make up Travellers Rest; the newly imagined historic herb garden will now be
a part of that same stewardship. Please see attachment “Garden Maintenance
Schedule” for a detailed maintenance plan.

4. Provide a plan for the distribution of items harvested.

In conjunction with the Tyne-Meade Garden Club Travellers Rest will offer a free garden
day event at the site. This will be promoted within our local community, as well as with the
greater Nashville area. Participants will learn about heirloom gardening practices, herb lore
and the history of herbs in 18" and 19" century America. To better share our efforts with
the community we will distribute seeds collected from our plants to the general public at this
event. In addition, we will also have samples of herb teas made from the garden and a
lecture on the benefits of herbal teas and the science behind historic herbal remedies.
Furthermore, we hope to offer weekend lectures and workshops to further explore the
history and modern-day relevance of herbs and heirloom gardening. The reimagined
historic herb garden will also be a core component of student focused education
programming. Schools will be able to “share” in the garden through seed planting activities,
garden exploration and other curriculum-based activities. With over 10,000 students visiting
Travellers Rest each year the herb garden will be a great educational resource for Middle
Tennessee children and educators.

Travellers Rest has a long and reputable history of providing quality historical education to
Middle Tennesseans of all ages for more than thirty years. As the city’s oldest historic
home open to the public and as a recognized leader in field trip education, Travellers Rest
provides the perfect setting to connect America’s gardening past to today’s generation.
