Diane,
We are excited about the opportunity to work with you and your staff at this moment in time. The February 
opening of the Family Justice Center marks a watershed moment for your office, and by extension, for the 
entire Nashville community. The hard work of the last several years and the vision you have championed take 
another big step forward as the Center comes to fruition, bringing all the associated agencies and departments 
together in a unifying facility.
The discussions we’ve had together have fueled our excitement. There is symmetry between your organization 
and ours, centered around the difficult task you take on: acting as guardian to those who have experienced 
domestic violence, child sexual abuse, sexual assault, and human trafficking. As you’ve seen in our previous 
work, we are attuned to these issues and the non-profit organizations who seek to serve the least of our 
neighbors. 
As we acknowledge in the opening line of the “Behind Closed Doors” script we shared with you, Trish, 
LaToya and Becky, Nashville is blessed. Your Office of Family Safety is one of the blessings. We have been 
impressed with your gifts and talents, your integrity, and your dedication and are grateful for the potential 
partnership we are building.
After touring the Family Justice Center and sharing our most recent discussions, we arrived at a point of 
agreement about next steps; the first of which was to share initial concepts with you as a good faith effort to 
make sure we were all on the same page should we move forward together. You and your team responded 
equally well to both the “Behind Closed Doors” concept and the “Time to Wake Up” concept. These short 
films are two of the four components of our proposal:
1. “FAMILY JUSTICE IN NASHVILLE”
A documentary style film that explores Nashville’s commitment to family safety and justice through the words of 
those that know the story best: the mayor’s office, law enforcement, the District Attorney’s office, the leadership 
of the Office of Family Safety, leaders of agencies housed at the Family Justice Center, and most importantly, 
Nashvillians who have survived domestic assault. These powerful testimonies, combined with compelling support 
footage, will share a story of healing and hope as we address an epidemic growing across all demographics in our 
community.
 
2. “BEHIND CLOSED DOORS”
A short narrative film (2-3 minutes) that becomes a key asset in the awareness campaign for the Family Justice 
Center, appealing to a broad audience that includes the general public, civic leaders, health and human services 
professionals, and those neighbors of ours who might know someone in the midst of a dangerous situation within 
their family or friendships. This is what we call a “statement” piece. The goal is to create a dramatic moment of 
awareness that results in people wanting to know more. The central metaphor in the piece is doors - front doors 
to all kinds of domiciles, doors of businesses, doors of hospitals, doors of the Family Justice Center. These doors 
can be mysterious and hide things, and they can welcome people - both powerful behaviors surrounding the pain 
as well as the healing and hope of this epidemic.

3. “TIME TO WAKE UP”
A 30-60 second, narrative piece with a similar style and content as “Behind Closed Doors”, yet with a different, 
more urgent metaphor: alarm clocks. The message for the community is that we must wake up before more of 
our neighbors become trapped in domestic violence and can’t see a way out. This piece is intended for use and 
distribution where time and an urgent message are crucial. 
4. BRANDED CONTENT LIBRARY
In the process of producing the documentary style “Family Justice in Nashville,” we will film a number of interviews 
with people who are intimately familiar with the issues of family safety and justice. Each of their full stories offer 
a unique perspective on the complex nature of this dilemma of human behavior that threatens our community. 
Therefore, we propose to create a library of these interviews, each a fully produced, standalone video of 3-5 
minutes in length. These interviews become rich content for use on the websites of the Office of Family Safety and 
the Family Justice Center, and can also be periodically rolled out on social media.
These four components comprise a set of branded content that can serve the Office of Family safety for years 
to come; providing a flexible set of powerful visual assets that increase awareness, support grant proposals, 
inform the community, create a call to action, provide a model for other cities, and offer a strong message 
to all Nashvillians that this issue of domestic violence has our attention and we are creating world class 
approaches and facilities to tackle this epidemic.
On a practical level, we understand that there is a tight time frame to deliver parts of this proposal for 
the February ribbon cutting ceremony that marks the official opening of the Family Justice Center. We are 
prepared to begin work as soon as this proposal is approved and the Office of Family Safety has the needed 
funding. 
The attached budget details the amount of the investment required for this project. We propose a timeline for 
payment, but we can be flexible on these terms in order to fit the needs of the project. Thank you, again, for 
your meaningful work for a important cause and for considering this proposal.
